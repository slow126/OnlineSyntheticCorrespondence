\subsection{Protocol overview}
\label{subsec:protocol_overview}

We evaluate transfer prediction on a dataset-transfer grid defined by candidate train datasets $T$ and target benchmarks $B$.
Each $(T,B)$ pair contributes observed transfer outcomes and precomputed discrepancy features, enabling utility prediction without full retraining for every design iteration.

\subsection{Target variable}
\label{subsec:target_variable}

The primary regression target is:
\begin{equation}
y(T,B)=\texttt{auc\_normalized\_observed}(T,B).
\end{equation}
This target reflects observed transfer quality over available training horizons and is used consistently in all main experiments.

\subsection{Validation design}
\label{subsec:validation_design}

We use two leakage-aware validation schemes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{LOTO (Leave-One-Train-dataset-Out):} tests generalization to unseen train-set constructions.
\item \textbf{LOBO (Leave-One-Benchmark-Out):} tests benchmark transfer.
\end{itemize}

For LOBO, we avoid benchmark-specific intercept leakage by not introducing held-out benchmark identity effects in the prediction fold.
This enforces true out-of-benchmark evaluation of utility.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}
\label{subsec:evaluation_metrics}

Primary metrics are error-based:
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
\item Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
\end{itemize}

Supporting association metrics:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pearson correlation
\item Spearman rank correlation
\end{itemize}

Ranking-oriented analyses (top-$k$, regret, rank error) are reported as optional/appendix diagnostics.

\subsection{Method-family comparison and parity}
\label{subsec:method_parity}

We compare directional, symmetric, and mixed predictor families under matched training/evaluation protocol.
Where applicable, parameter parity is enforced (same target, same split logic, same preprocessing policy), and any family-specific hyperparameters are isolated.
Full grid-search ranges and model-selection details are deferred to the appendix for reproducibility.
