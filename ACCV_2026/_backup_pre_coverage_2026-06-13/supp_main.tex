\subsection{Where directionality does \emph{not} live: the anchor}
Directional borrowing of the level anchor $L$ (interpolating observed cells
with regime-matched directed similarities) \emph{loses} to plain symmetric
inverse-distance weighting on both anchor-interpolation problems
(held-out-benchmark MAE 16.9 symmetric vs.\ 18.9--24.1 directional;
held-out-source 8.6 vs.\ 8.7--10.1). Directionality lives where learning
happens (the ranking term $g$), not where measurement is interpolated---a
clean separation between the principle and the plumbing.

\subsection{Pretrained arm of the generated grid}
The pretrained (frozen-backbone) arm of the intervention grid spans nine
sources, but the grid was constructed to vary off-target mass, so the
missing-support distance is near-constant across these sources and any ranking
it induces is estimator-noise-limited. Its unstable point estimates nominally
favor the off-target direction ($\dtb$ $+0.64$, $\dbt$ $+0.21$, sym $+0.37$)
---the \emph{opposite} of the pretrained prediction---which we report
undiscounted rather than omit, and we do not treat this arm as a test of the
pretrained half. A coverage-varying pretrained grid (varying $\dbt$ at fixed
$\dtb$) is the natural follow-up experiment.

\subsection{BFV construct validity: full recovered parameters}
Table~\ref{tab:recovery_full} gives the complete recovered generator
parameters behind main-paper Fig.~7. The camera dolly magnitude and the object
vertical-motion fraction are the two axes that separate the regimes; the
remaining object-motion parameters (translation, rotation, number of movers)
are reported for completeness. \textbf{The known blind spot:} BFV aggregates
flow vectors and is therefore invariant to whether a given aggregate field was
produced by a moving camera or by correspondingly moving objects---a lateral
camera pan and a lateral object slide yield near-identical BFV histograms. This
is a feature of the analysis, not a failure: the descriptor recovers
\emph{aggregate} motion (the quantity that drives transfer) faithfully, and is
knowably agnostic to the camera/object factorization that produces it. A
target whose motion is dominated by camera/object ambiguity (e.g.\ a pure
lateral pan) is recovered as the corresponding object motion---the same
aggregate field, a different mechanism. Disentangling the two would require a
descriptor that encodes scene geometry, which is beyond the scope of this
motion-only study.

\section{Sampling Stability of the Estimators}
\label{supp:sampling}

We recompute every feature at flow-vector budgets from $5{\times}10^4$ to
$8{\times}10^6$ and correlate each budget's value against the largest
budget's, across all 110 train$\to$benchmark pairs. Mean-NN distances in both
directions reach $\rho \ge 0.96$ by $5{\times}10^4$ vectors and $1.000$ at
the reference budget; $\epsilon$-coverage at 4\,px behaves similarly. In
contrast, kNN-KL estimators plateau near $\rho{=}0.5$ ($T{\to}B$) and
$\rho{=}0.2$ ($B{\to}T$) and never stabilize---they measure density, which
for procedurally generated data is an artifact of the sampling budget rather
than an intrinsic property. This motivates the main paper's restriction to
support-geometry estimators. Within the near-tied intervention family, the
$\dtb$ ranking remains estimator-noise-limited even after 5-seed averaging;
out-of-sample correlations there are attenuated (conservative), and the
canonical training sets, whose spreads are an order of magnitude wider, are
unaffected.

\section{The Middlebury Exclusion}
\label{supp:mid}

During analysis we observed that for pretrained variants the Middlebury
evaluation returned $52.6 \pm 4.7$ \pck{} regardless of training source
(within-context spread 4.7 vs.\ 10--17 on other benchmarks), while
from-scratch variants spread normally (15.3). Inspection identified a defect
in our Middlebury evaluation harness; pending re-evaluation, all reported
numbers exclude Middlebury (the remaining 9 benchmarks; 99 models, 891
evaluations). Sensitivity: with Middlebury included, every headline conclusion
is unchanged---the flip statistic is $+0.82$ rather than $+0.90$, and the
policy's held-out correlations shift by at most ${\sim}0.03$; the largest
single effect of the exclusion is on the pretrained-regime oracle fraction
($0.554\!\to\!0.612$), because
near-invariant contexts are unrankable and dilute within-context averages.

\section{Additional Robustness and Disentangling Analyses}
\label{supp:newrobust}
This section collects diagnostics that probe how far the principle generalizes
and separate the two confounds---transfer level and appearance---from the
directed-motion signal. All use the nine-benchmark (Middlebury-free) table.

\subsection{Is the binding direction carried by specific datasets?}
We jackknife the regime-mean directed correlations, dropping each source
(leave-one-source-out, $\times 11$) and each generator family
(leave-one-family-out, $\times 5$), and report the range of the
regime-relevant direction. The pretrained missing-support signal is
dataset-independent (range $[{+}0.57,{+}0.65]$, no family load-bearing); the
from-scratch off-target signal is real but softer and leans on the semantic
source (spair): dropping it halves the correlation, though it never flips sign.
This is why we present the pretrained half as the robust core and the
from-scratch off-target lean as a weaker, source-dependent effect.

\caption{Per-regime source/family jackknife. ``Span'' is the spread of the
regime-relevant correlation over all 16 single drops.}
\label{tab:new_jackknife}
\end{table}

\subsection{Appearance vs.\ motion: partial correlations}
Within each context we partial the directed motion and appearance (DINO)
distances against one another. Motion's ranking signal survives controlling
for appearance (pretrained recall $+0.61\!\to\!+0.54$; scratch symmetric
$+0.50\!\to\!+0.51$), so it is not an appearance confound. Appearance distance
controlling for motion is wrong-signed ($-0.20$ to $-0.36$)---no usable ranking
signal, consistent with the synthetic$\to$real distance being dominated by a
near-constant domain gap.
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\input{tables/tab_new_partial}
\caption{Raw and partial within-context Spearman correlations of transfer with
the directed motion and appearance distances.}
\label{tab:new_partial}
\end{table}

\subsection{Fixed-motion appearance: level vs.\ selection}
Four appearance variants of one camera-dominant source (mesh $\times$ texture)
share motion (flow-distance spread $\le 1.03\times$). Within each benchmark,
appearance distance orders transfer ($\rho$ up to $+0.80$)---but the
\emph{same} variant wins on every benchmark (cross-benchmark ordering
$\rho \approx +0.8$ to $+1.0$). Appearance therefore sets a target-invariant
\emph{level}, not a target-selective ranking; the within-benchmark correlation
is the level effect, since realistic texture is uniformly both better and
appearance-closer.
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\input{tables/tab_new_fixedmotion}
\caption{Peak PCK of four fixed-motion appearance variants across benchmarks.
Within-benchmark $\rho$(pck,$-$appearance dist.) is $+0.60/+0.80/+0.80$; the
cross-benchmark ordering correlation is $+0.80/+1.00/+0.80$---the same variant
wins everywhere (level, not selection).}
\label{tab:new_fixedmotion}
\end{table}

\subsection{The flip is regime, not transfer level}
At the variant grain regime and mean transfer level are nearly collinear; at
the context grain (variant $\times$ benchmark, $n{=}81$) they separate.
Regressing the per-context direction gap ($\rho[\dbt]-\rho[\dtb]$) on a regime
indicator and level, regime is strongly significant and level is not, and
within the pretrained regime the gap--level association is \emph{negative}---the
opposite of a dynamic-range artifact.
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\input{tables/tab_new_regimelevel}
\caption{Deconfounding the direction flip from transfer level. $R^2$ is
$0.41$ regime-only vs $0.11$ level-only; within the pretrained regime
$\rho$(gap,level)$=-0.41$ (scratch $+0.42$).}
\label{tab:new_regimelevel}
\end{table}

\subsection{Which OOS cells are interpretable}
On the generated grid, a directed correlation is interpretable only where its
distance has dynamic range. The from-scratch FlyingThings off-target cell has
large spread and is rank-stable; the pretrained KITTI precision cells, despite
large $\rho$, have $\le 4\%$ distance spread and collapse under $5\%$
perturbation (rank self-correlation $0.15$--$0.28$)---so they cannot support a
conclusion. The pretrained FlyingThings precision cell ($+0.73$, $p{=}0.03$,
$139\%$ spread) is the exception we flag in the main text.

\input{tables/tab_new_ttarm}

relative distance spread, and rank self-correlation under $5\%$ noise.}

\end{table}

The intervention-grid distances are five-seed averages. Re-generating them at
each seed leaves the headline FlyingThings off-target correlation stable
($+0.58$ to $+0.67$), so the pre-registered result is not an averaging
artifact.

\subsection{Heterogeneity of the off-target/missing-support trade-off}
The two directed distances are anti-correlated on average ($-0.37$), but the
strength varies sharply by benchmark---strong on semantic/TSS targets, near
zero on KITTI-2015 and SPair, and \emph{positive} on KITTI-2012. The
``averaging corrupts'' mechanism is therefore benchmark-dependent.
