\section{Interventions: Steering Generation and Stress-Testing Coverage}
\label{sec:interv}

We stress-test the coverage finding with interventions, probing three
questions on a procedurally generated grid (Kubric-derived motion interventions
\cite{greff2021kubric} plus our SDF-Fractals3D generator, an asset-free procedural
implicit-surface pipeline that shares no rendering code or asset library with
Kubric), constructed after the
analysis of Secs.~\ref{sec:law}--\ref{sec:absolute} was frozen: \emph{(i)}
construct validity, \emph{(ii)} whether a tuned source beats a generic baseline,
and \emph{(iii)} whether motion (not appearance) is the target-specific carrier
and the coverage response has the predicted shape.

\noindent\textbf{(i) The recovered motion is physically plausible
(construct validity).}
We run a geometry-only search over generator motion parameters, scored only by how
well the generated motion covers the target's, and ask what motion matching the
descriptor produces (\emph{geometry-only}: each candidate costs only a
flow pass over the scene's motion field, with no texture or lighting render and no
network training; only the winning source is later materialized in full, with
appearance, to train on and verify in (ii)).
On a
controllable Kubric-style generator whose parameters $\theta$ explicitly
separate camera motion from object motion, optimizing BFV match alone recovers
the expected motion regime for each target (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}): a strong
forward dolly with grounded objects for
KITTI (camera-centric), a near-static camera with vertically flying objects for
FlyingThings (object-centric). The recovered camera axis separates the
moving-camera target from the static-camera target, with no supervision beyond
the motion histogram. The recovered KITTI source's flow fingerprint bears this
out, a coherent radial-expansion field that captures the target's
forward-ego-motion structure, unlike the isotropic generic anchor
(Fig.~\ref{fig:intervsplats}). An independent generator, matched on BFV alone,
lands on the right physical motion (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}); because the
objective fixes only the aggregate motion histogram, we read the camera/object
split off the recovered $\theta$, with its real test following in (ii).

\noindent\textbf{(ii) A tuned source beats a generic baseline.}
The recovered motion also trains well. Materializing the KITTI-recovered source
(GSO assets, HDRI lighting) and training on it beats MOVi-F~\cite{greff2021kubric}
in \emph{all eight} KITTI settings, with gains of $+1.6$ to $+18.7$ \pck{}
across the four architectures (Table~\ref{tab:interv}). This source was built
without a single real-target label.

\noindent\textbf{The objective is renderer-agnostic.}
The same geometry-only coverage search, run on our SDF-Fractals3D generator (which
shares no rendering code or assets with Kubric), produces a KITTI source whose
tuned motion improves on the generator's default across the \pck{} threshold ladder,
mirroring the Kubric result in the same table
(Table~\ref{tab:interv}). Two structurally unrelated generators, tuned to
the same target by the same motion-coverage objective, arrive at the same motion, so
the rule generalizes across renderers.

\noindent\textbf{(iii) Motion is the carrier, and the coverage response is
inverted-U.} Scaling camera-dolly magnitude across five rungs ($0.25\times$ to
$2\times$ KITTI's) and crossing with four texture variants (two appearance
knobs, lighting and assets; gallery in the supplement), we train GLU-Net from
scratch on each of the $20$ sources and read peak \pck{} on KITTI
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). Transfer is \emph{inverted-U} in magnitude, peaking at
the matched rung for both KITTI splits and every texture variant; missing the
match costs $5$--$11$ \pck. Off-target mass ($\dtb$) stays flat across the ladder
while coverage ($\dbt$) tracks the PCK curve, so coverage is the operative
lever. Because magnitude moves both directions together, this maps the coverage
\emph{response} rather than isolating it; the flat $\dtb$ indicates the off-target
direction is not what drives the curve. Appearance leaves the curve's shape
unchanged: even the most realistic variant, HDRI lighting with real scanned (GSO)
objects, gives no consistent edge over flat-shaded procedural primitives
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}), confirming that motion, rather than realism or object
content, is the target-specific carrier.
