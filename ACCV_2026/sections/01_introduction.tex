\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dense visual correspondence (optical flow, geometric and semantic matching)
relies heavily on synthetic training data, because dense ground truth is scarce
on real images
\cite{mayer2016FlyingThingsDataset,sintel,greff2021kubric,pointodyssey}. Most
progress targets \emph{how} to render this data (fidelity, lighting, speed)
\cite{greff2021kubric,pointodyssey}, on the assumption that closing the
appearance gap drives transfer. Yet non-photorealistic data often transfers well
\cite{Baradad2021LookingAtNoise,white2009mandelbulb}, and a logically prior
question gets far less attention: given a controllable pipeline, \emph{what}
should it render for a given target? As rendering becomes more efficient and the
appearance gap narrows, what to generate becomes the binding constraint.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/concept_directional_vs_symmetric.png}
\caption{\textbf{Why direction matters.} A conceptual two-mode example.
\textbf{(A)}~\emph{Under-coverage}: the target places mass on a mode the source
never renders; coverage ($\dbt$, recall-analogue) drops while off-target mass
stays zero.
\textbf{(B)}~\emph{Excess mass}: the mirror case; off-target ($\dtb$,
precision-analogue) is large, coverage is perfect.
\textbf{(C)}~The directed measures flip between cases and identify which failure
occurred; a symmetric distance is nearly identical for both.}
\label{fig:concept}
\end{figure}

We find that the match between source and target is inherently directional,
and that the two directions are not equivalent.
A source may fail to cover motions the target needs
(\textbf{coverage}; recall-analogous) or render motion the target never uses
(\textbf{off-target mass}; precision-analogous); we measure these as the two
directed nearest-neighbor distances between each dataset's flow-vector
distributions (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}).
Coverage is the signal: it is the consistent predictor of transfer across
architectures, benchmarks, and distance estimators.
Off-target mass is nearly free---a source can render motion the target never uses
at negligible transfer cost---so symmetric distances such as
FID~\cite{heusel2018ganstrainedtimescaleupdate} and sliced Wasserstein, which
average both directions, dilute the signal rather than adding information.

Our evidence comes from 76 trained correspondence models: four architecture
families (CATs++ \cite{cho2022cats++}, RAFT \cite{teed2020raft},
GLU-Net \cite{truong2020glunet}, and FlowFormer \cite{huang2022flowformer}) in
seven backbone configurations, trained over 11 sources and evaluated on 9
benchmarks ($684$ transfer measurements). We stress-test the coverage finding
from several angles: replication across three distance estimators, a negative
control in appearance-feature space, a render-free construct-validity check in
which matching the motion descriptor alone recovers each target's expected motion
regime, and a controllable grid crossing motion against appearance on which a
target-tuned source beats a generic baseline.

We make the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Coverage is the signal} (Sect.~\ref{sec:law}): motion coverage
  ($\dbt$) is the most reliable predictor of correspondence transfer across all
  four architecture families and nine benchmarks, consistently outperforming
  symmetric distances and appearance-based alternatives.
\item \textbf{A render-free selection rule} (Sects.~\ref{sec:predict}--\ref{sec:absolute}):
  ranking candidate sources by $\dbt$ requires no target labels, no training, and
  no differentiable renderer, yet picks a source close to the best at zero shot.
  A few-shot extension given a handful of anchor runs also predicts absolute \pck{}.
\item \textbf{Off-target mass is nearly free} (Sects.~\ref{sec:law}--\ref{sec:predict}):
  a source can render motions the target never uses at little transfer cost.
  Symmetric distances fold off-target mass back in and dilute the coverage signal;
  the practical rule is to \emph{cover} the target's motion rather than match its
  full distribution.
\item \textbf{Interventional support} (Sect.~\ref{sec:interv}): a render-free
  generator search scored only on motion coverage recovers each target's expected
  motion regime---a forward camera dolly for KITTI, a near-static camera for the
  semantic TSS benchmark---and the tuned source beats a generic Kubric baseline on
  both (all fourteen KITTI settings and all three TSS). A controlled
  motion-vs-appearance grid confirms that motion, not appearance, is the
  target-specific carrier of transfer.
\end{enumerate}
