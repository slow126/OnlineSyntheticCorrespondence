\noindent\textbf{Selecting training data zero-shot.}
\label{sec:predict}
Table~\ref{tab:predictors} pools each distance's ranking power across the transfer
study. Motion coverage ($\dbt$) ranks at $\rho{=}0.57$, the best predictor in every
column. The symmetric distribution distances follow at $+0.39$--$0.40$ (Chamfer,
sliced Wasserstein-2, FID). They trail coverage because each folds in off-target mass
($\dtb$), which on its own carries little signal ($+0.03$), so averaging it in adds
noise. Despite DINOv3's strong zero-shot matching, its distances are negligible to
anti-correlated with transfer.

By the median top-1 regret, ranking by motion coverage gives up only $0.6$ \pck{}
against the true best source, so its top pick is the best source or a near-tie.
Ranking by appearance coverage gives up $11.5$, close to the $15.1$ of a random pick
(Table~\ref{tab:predictors}).

The positive and stable correlation of motion coverage suggests a \emph{zero-shot}
selection rule with no fitted or trained parameters:
\begin{equation}
\text{rank candidate training sources by motion coverage,}\qquad \dbt .
\label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
It needs no training run on the target and no learned weights, only the target's motion
statistics.

\noindent\textbf{Coverage recovers most of the data-attributable signal.} We estimate a
tight upper-bound for the data-attributable signal by retraining the same architecture on
the same target under different backbone configurations (frozen or fine-tuned) and measuring the consensus of their source rankings. We find the upper bound to be $\rho{=}0.62$, with the remaining variance being attributable to model-specific effects. These model-specific effects are beyond the
reach of any data-only rule, and we find that motion coverage at $\rho{=}0.57$ is $92\%$ of this upper bound
(pairwise-decision view, Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_law_b}).

Learned combiners (coverage$\times$off-target interactions, gradient-boosted
ensembles, fit in-fold) do not improve over our zero-shot coverage
(see supplementary Sect.~\ref{S-supp:predict}). The full
source$\times$target landscape is in the supplement (supp. Fig.~\ref{S-fig:consensus}).
