\subsection{Selecting Training Data at the Reproducibility Ceiling}
\label{sec:predict}

The coverage trend supports a \emph{zero-shot} selection rule with zero fitted
parameters:
\begin{equation}
\text{rank candidate training sets by motion coverage,}\qquad -\dbt .
\label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
It requires no run on the target, no labels, and no learned weights (unlike the
few-shot absolute predictor of Sect.~\ref{sec:absolute}), and it ranks candidates
at the reproducibility ceiling of transfer itself (below). It is also
the right generator-\emph{design} objective: because off-target motion is nearly
free (precision $\rho{=}0.03$), one should \emph{add} coverage of the target's
motion rather than \emph{match} its distribution---the move our geometry-only
search exploits (Sect.~\ref{sec:interv}).

Table~\ref{tab:predictors} runs the same five distances in motion and in
appearance space, so appearance is stress-tested as thoroughly as motion, not
given a single shot. Motion coverage ($\dbt$) ranks at $\rho{=}0.57$---the best
predictor in every column. The symmetric and distribution distances follow at
$+0.39$--$0.40$ (symmetric Chamfer, sliced Wasserstein-2, FID): they trail
coverage because each folds in off-target mass ($\dtb$), which on its own carries
little signal ($+0.03$), so averaging it in is dead weight rather than denoising.
Appearance is the negative control---and a serious one: DINOv3 captures object
identity, texture, and semantic category---exactly the content one would align a
source to a target on---and its strong zero-shot matching made it our leading
hypothesis at the outset. Every DINO distance is
null-to-wrong-signed, the appearance \emph{analogue} of coverage included
($-0.26$). The axis that selects \emph{which} target a source suits lives in
motion; appearance sets only the source's overall quality.

\noindent\textbf{Coverage recovers most of the data-controllable signal.} The
empirical oracle---how well same-family retrainings under different backbone
configurations agree on source ordering---reaches only $\rho{\approx}0.62$:
transfer is itself imperfectly reproducible, and that residual is model-specific
variance out of reach of any data-only rule. Coverage at $\rho{=}0.57$ is
\textbf{$92\%$ of this ceiling} (pairwise-decision view:
Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_law_b}).

Learned combiners (coverage$\times$off-target interactions, gradient-boosted
ensembles, fit in-fold) add nothing over fit-free coverage
(Table~\ref{tab:predictors}, supp.).

\noindent\textbf{What a practitioner gains.} Ranking by coverage gives up a median
$0.6$ \pck{} versus $15.1$ for random (Table~\ref{tab:predictors}); below a
$2$-\pck{} gap every strategy, retraining included, decays toward chance.
Figure~\ref{fig:consensus} lays out the full source$\times$target landscape.
