\section{Interventions: Steering Generation and Stress-Testing Coverage}
\label{sec:interv}

A correlational principle should be stressed interventionally. Every result so
far draws on one observational table, so this section asks four questions of
data that table never touched: a procedurally generated grid (Kubric-derived
motion interventions \cite{greff2021kubric} plus our SDF-fractal generator),
constructed after the analysis of Secs.~\ref{sec:law}--\ref{sec:absolute} was
frozen. \emph{(i)~Is the descriptor meaningful?} Does matching it alone recover
each target's physically correct motion? \emph{(ii)~Is tuning actionable?} Does
a source tuned to cover a target beat a generic synthetic baseline?
\emph{(iii)~Is motion, not appearance, the carrier, and does its coverage
response have the predicted shape?} For (iii) we build a controlled
motion-magnitude $\times$ appearance grid under one matched training protocol
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). Scaling magnitude moves coverage and off-target mass
together, so this maps the coverage \emph{response curve} rather than isolating
coverage at fixed off-target mass; that cleaner isolation remains the one
experiment we flag as residual (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).
\emph{(iv)~Is off-target mass actually free?} The complementary ladder---injecting
off-target motion while holding coverage pinned---tests the directional claim
head-on.

\noindent\textbf{(i) The recovered motion is physically plausible
(construct validity).} A descriptor as crude as a 4-D bag of flow vectors
invites a natural worry: does it capture anything real? We test this directly.
We run a render-free search over generator motion parameters, scored only by how
well the generated motion covers the target's, and ask what motion matching the
descriptor actually produces (\emph{render-free}: each candidate costs only a
geometry-only flow pass---the scene's motion field, with no texture or lighting
render---and no training; only the winning source is later materialized in full,
with appearance, to train on and verify in (ii)).
On a
controllable Kubric-style generator whose parameters $\theta$ explicitly
separate camera motion from object motion, optimizing BFV match alone recovers
the expected motion regime for each target (Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery},
Table~\ref{tab:recovery}): a strong forward dolly with grounded objects for
KITTI (camera-centric), a near-static camera with vertically flying objects for
FlyingThings (object-centric). The recovered camera axis cleanly separates the
moving-camera target from the static-camera target, with no supervision beyond
the motion histogram. The recovered KITTI source's flow fingerprint bears this
out, a coherent radial-expansion field that captures the target's
forward-ego-motion structure, unlike the isotropic generic anchor
(Fig.~\ref{fig:intervsplats}). An independent generator, matched on BFV alone,
lands on the right physical motion: construct validity earned, not asserted. One
caveat: BFV is by construction blind to the camera-vs-object \emph{decomposition}
when the two produce identical aggregate flow, since an ego-motion dolly and
flying objects can share an aggregate fingerprint. We therefore read the
camera/object split off $\theta$, not off the splat, and treat the ambiguity in
the supplement.

\noindent\textbf{(ii) A tuned source beats a generic baseline.}
The recovered motion is not just physically plausible; it also trains well. We
materialize the KITTI-recovered source (GSO assets, HDRI lighting) and train on
it. It matches or beats MOVi-F \cite{greff2021kubric}, the standard generic
Kubric baseline, in \emph{all twelve} KITTI settings across three architectures
(Table~\ref{tab:interv}). CATs++ improves by $+8.3$/$+1.9$ from scratch and
$+2.7$/$+1.8$ with a pretrained backbone; GLU-Net improves by $+13.3$/$+7.0$
from scratch and by a striking $+22.5$/$+16.1$ with a pretrained backbone; and
FlowFormer improves by $+10.9$/$+3.5$ from scratch and $+18.7$/$+10.1$ with a
pretrained backbone (KITTI-2012/2015). The two CATs++-scratch wins come from prior-matching, not
learned features: that configuration is off-design (supp.),
so a source whose motion marginal sits closer to the target transfers better
even though the encoder never learns (Sec.~\ref{sec:law}). We compare source and
baseline at their best checkpoint (peak \pck) under matched training, so neither
is penalized by schedule length. A target-specific source built without a single
real-target label thus beats a generic synthetic dataset.

\noindent\textbf{(iii) Motion is the carrier, and the coverage response is
inverted-U.} The experiments above turn on motion \emph{type}; we close by varying
motion \emph{amount}. Holding scene, objects, and training protocol fixed, we
scale the camera-dolly magnitude across five rungs ($0.25\times$ to $2\times$
KITTI's), cross it with texture, train GLU-Net from scratch on each of the $20$
sources, and read peak \pck{} on KITTI (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). Transfer is
\emph{inverted-U} in magnitude, peaking at the matched rung ($1\times$) for both
KITTI splits and every texture variant, and it tracks coverage directly: the source
covers KITTI's motions best at the match and worse on either side
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}, left).
\emph{Under-coverage} ($0.25\times$) is too slow to reach KITTI's larger motions;
\emph{over-shoot} ($2\times$) is too fast, so its motion mass shifts off KITTI's
actual range and coverage falls (the source's overlap with KITTI drops from
$30\%$ at the match to $15\%$ at $2\times$). Missing the target's motion in either
direction costs $5$--$11$ \pck. The cost is dominated by lost coverage: off-target
mass rises only mildly across the over-shoot arm, so this grid maps the coverage
response and points to coverage as the lever. Magnitude alone moves both directions
at once; the fixed-coverage ladder in (iv) separates them, confirming off-target
mass is nearly free, while the complementary test---varying coverage breadth at
fixed off-target mass---remains future work (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}). The reading reinforces the design rule: a source must
\emph{cover} the target's motion, which over-shooting fails to do as surely as
under-shooting. Appearance, by contrast, only shifts the level: every appearance
variant shows the same inverted-U in magnitude, the HDRI background sitting a
near-constant step above matte at every rung (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). It sets a
source's overall quality, not which magnitude transfers best, so motion---not
appearance---is the target-specific carrier. (From scratch, matte cannot fully
absorb a gross over-shoot, matte/$2\times$ falling to $82.9$ on KITTI-2012 vs.\
photoreal's $91.6$; a pretrained backbone restores the tolerance, supp.)

\noindent\textbf{(iv) Off-target mass is nearly free---a direct test at fixed
coverage.} The magnitude grid moves coverage and off-target together; here we move
\emph{only} off-target. Starting from the KITTI-recovered source we replace a
fraction $f$ of training pairs with horizontally-mirrored copies, injecting
strongly off-target motion while leaving coverage pinned---the unflipped $1{-}f$
pairs still span KITTI's motion, and the measured BFV ladder confirms it ($\dbt$
flat at ${\approx}0.0017$ while $\dtb$ ramps $16\times$). Training on the ladder
(supp.; the from-scratch CATs++ config is off-design),
off-target mass is
nearly free: pushing half the training motion off-target ($f{=}0.5$) costs at most
$-2.4$ \pck{} (GLU-Net from scratch) and is flat under a pretrained backbone
($-1.3$ CATs++, $+0.3$ GLU-Net). With coverage held fixed, adding off-target motion
barely moves transfer in every regime: the directional claim's missing
causal leg. One caveat by construction: mirror motion is unnatural, so this bounds
off-target as sensitivity to \emph{degenerate} off-target motion rather than a
natural co-equal cost---for a broad-support target like KITTI, natural off-target
mass is hard to construct.
