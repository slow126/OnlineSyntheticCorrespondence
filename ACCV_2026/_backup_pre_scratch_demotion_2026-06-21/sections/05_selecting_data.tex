\subsection{Selecting Training Data at the Reproducibility Ceiling}
\label{sec:predict}

The coverage law licenses a \emph{zero-shot} selection rule with zero fitted
parameters:
\begin{equation}
\text{rank candidate training sets by motion coverage,}\qquad -\dbt .
\label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
It is zero-shot in the strict sense: no run on the target, no target labels, no
learned weights, and nothing fit and held out (unlike the absolute predictor of
Sec.~\ref{sec:absolute}). It is a single directed distance computed from each
candidate's motion histogram against the target's.
Ranking is what coverage gives for free; \emph{absolute} \pck{} needs a few
observed runs as anchors (the few-shot extension, Sec.~\ref{sec:absolute}).
Across the configurations we analyze (each architecture in its designed regime,
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}), coverage is the best predictor we have, and it ranks
candidates at the reproducibility ceiling of transfer itself (below). It is also
the right generator-\emph{design} objective: because off-target motion is nearly
free (precision $\rho{=}0.03$), one should \emph{add} coverage of the target's
motion rather than \emph{match} its distribution---the move our render-free
search exploits (Sec.~\ref{sec:interv}).

Table~\ref{tab:predictors} runs the same five distances in motion and in
appearance space, so appearance is stress-tested as thoroughly as motion, not
given a single shot. Motion coverage ($\dbt$) ranks at $\rho{=}0.57$---the best
predictor in every column. The symmetric and distribution distances follow at
$+0.39$--$0.40$ (symmetric mean-NN, sliced Wasserstein-2, FID): they trail
coverage because each folds in off-target mass ($\dtb$), which on its own carries
little signal ($+0.03$), so averaging it in is dead weight rather than denoising.
Appearance is the negative control: every DINO distance is null-to-wrong-signed,
the appearance \emph{analogue} of coverage included ($-0.26$). The axis
that selects \emph{which} target a source suits lives in motion; appearance sets
only the source's overall quality.
Reported in each architecture's designed regime, coverage is $+0.57$; pooling in
the off-design from-scratch configurations would dilute it to $+0.35$ (coverage
cannot rank sources for a model that ignores its input), while the symmetric
metrics barely move---the precision term they carry is unhelpful throughout.

\noindent\textbf{Is $\rho{\approx}0.5$ good? It recovers most of the
data-controllable signal.} Transfer rank varies for two reasons: which
\emph{source} you train on, the part a dataset descriptor can see, and which
\emph{model} you train (architecture, initialization, schedule, size), which it
structurally cannot. We bound the first by how well independent retrainings
\emph{agree} on the source ordering: two models trained on the same sources rank
them alike only at $\rho{\approx}0.62$ (mean pairwise rank-agreement across
independent retrainings---the \emph{empirical oracle}). The variance above that
agreement is not predictor weakness; it is signal that lives in the model, not
the data, and is out of scope for any data-only selection rule. Coverage reaches
$\rho{=}0.57$, \textbf{$92\%$ of this data-controllable ceiling}: ranking
candidates by one motion distance recovers most of what retraining a sibling
model would tell you, at zero training cost and without rendering a frame.

We also probed for structure a single coverage distance might miss---learned
ridges over the full motion descriptor set, a coverage$\times$off-target
interaction, and a depth-2 gradient-boosted combiner, all fit in-fold and
applied held-out. None beats fit-free coverage (Table~\ref{tab:predictors}, and
supplement): there is nothing for a learned combiner to add over the one
directed distance. Ranking quality translates directly into the decision that
matters---which single source to train on (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_law_b}).

\noindent\textbf{What a practitioner gains.}
This is the difference between a near-optimal pick and a coin flip: ranking by
coverage gives up a median $0.6$ \pck{} versus $15.1$ for random
(Table~\ref{tab:predictors}), and on the gaps that matter it orders sources as
reliably as an independent retraining (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_law_b})---all zero-shot. Below a
$2$-\pck{} gap every strategy decays toward chance, \emph{retraining included}, so
the residual is measurement noise, not predictor deficiency.
Figure~\ref{fig:consensus} lays out the full source$\times$target landscape behind
these decisions.
