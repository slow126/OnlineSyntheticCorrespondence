Table~\ref{tab:law} and Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_law_a} present the core finding.
\emph{Coverage} ($\dbt$) is the consistent positive predictor, up to $+0.73$ on
real motion and, under a pretrained backbone, on semantic targets too (CATs++
$+0.50$, GLU-Net $+0.80$). It is the best
predictor in every real-motion cell and stays positive on semantic targets
($+0.31$ to $+0.80$). On semantic targets it no longer dominates: a symmetric or
sliced-$W_2$ distance is competitive and, for FlowFormer, clearly better ($W_2$
$+0.65$ vs.\ coverage $+0.31$). We read this as semantic matching asking for more
than motion coverage---the full-distribution compatibility the symmetric distances
partly carry---consistent with our scope that motion descriptors capture coverage,
not full semantic compatibility (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}). Across the whole table,
though, coverage remains the reliable default: positive in every kept cell,
best-or-tied in the large majority, and the one distance worth reaching for without
knowing the regime, whereas off-target mass ($\dtb$) is erratic and near-zero on
its own. The source
ranking this produces for every target, set against realized transfer, is laid
out in Fig.~\ref{fig:consensus} below. In DINOv3 appearance space the identical
analysis is null throughout (Table~\ref{tab:lawdino}): the target-selective signal
lives in motion, not in how the frames look.


\noindent\textbf{Robustness.} Coverage's lead replicates across distance
estimators and generator families, and is not explained by dataset size (ranking
by \#samples or \#vectors reaches only $\rho\le0.27$). The supplement gives the
full per-configuration tables and all ablations: the two alternative estimators
($\epsilon$-radius coverage and a density kNN-KL), generator and sampling
controls, the direction-blind pooling analysis, and the spurious off-target
correlation confined to the off-design from-scratch cells.
