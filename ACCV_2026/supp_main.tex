\section{From-Scratch Training: An Out-of-Distribution Probe}
\label{supp:designcut}

The main analysis studies each backbone matcher (CATs++, GLU-Net, FlowFormer)
with its pretrained image-feature backbone, and RAFT---which has no such
backbone---end-to-end (main Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}). This section documents the from-scratch
configurations of the three backbone matchers, in which the feature extractors are
randomly initialized, in full. We treat them as out-of-distribution for two
reasons, established below: (i) removing pretrained features does \emph{not} make
the model lean on appearance---an appearance distance gains no predictive power
from scratch---so the probe's motivating hypothesis fails; and (ii) the
randomly-initialized backbones mode-collapse to a near-static prior that ignores
the image pair, so no data-selection signal can exist there, in either motion or
appearance. We confirm (ii) with two motion-free checks (source-sensitivity and
the encoder-gain test $\Delta_{\text{enc}}$). This set was fixed before any motion
distance was computed, so it is not an outcome-based exclusion.

\noindent\textbf{Appearance gains no predictive power from scratch.} The premise
behind training the matchers from scratch is that a randomly-initialized backbone,
lacking pretrained semantic features, might force the model to lean on raw
appearance instead---making an appearance distance newly predictive of which
target a source suits. It does not. In DINO appearance space the from-scratch
configurations are as null-to-wrong-signed as the pretrained ones
(Table~\ref{tab:lawdino_full}), and motion coverage remains the only positive
signal wherever the encoder learns at all. Removing pretrained features removes
predictive power; it does not relocate it to appearance.

\noindent\textbf{Source-sensitivity exposes the collapse.} Holding the target
image fixed, we swap the source image and record how far the predicted flow field
moves relative to its own magnitude---its \emph{source-sensitivity}. A genuine
matcher must move a great deal (a different source implies a different
correspondence); a model collapsed to a fixed field barely moves.
Table~\ref{tab:staticprior} reports it for the from-scratch backbone-dependent
matchers, averaged by target type and training-source type. The working matchers
on real motion (GLU-Net/FlowFormer) set the healthy scale at $5$--$9$; on semantic
targets every model falls to $\le0.5$, and under the semantic source to
${\approx}0.1$. The failed models stop tracking their input.

\section{Per-Configuration Evidence and the Pooled Flip}
\label{supp:law}

An earlier version of this analysis reported a pooled ``flip'' statistic---which
single direction predicts better, averaged over configurations. We document it
here, and show it is an artifact of pooling real-motion and semantic benchmarks
(main Sec.~\ref{sec:law}): once the from-scratch$\times$semantic regime is separated
out, there is no flip to explain. We give the per-configuration evidence
together with the flip statistic and its (genuinely tight) robustness, so the
artifact is documented rather than hidden. The takeaway matches the main text:
coverage is the consistent predictor under a backbone, the symmetric metrics
hedge, and the pooled flip is real \emph{as a pooled statistic} but is not a
separate law.

\subsection{Per-configuration coverage vs.\ symmetric, all metrics}
Table~\ref{tab:supp_asym} reports, per configuration, the regime-matched
coverage correlation against both fixed directions and three symmetric baselines
(the symmetric mean-NN average, FID, and sliced Wasserstein-2, all in the
same BFV motion space). We also evaluated MMD but exclude it throughout, as its
kernel estimate was unstable across bandwidths and sampling budgets. Coverage's margin over the symmetric metrics is largest
exactly where the two directions disagree most (pretrained GLU-Net), and the
symmetric metrics hedge everywhere---consistent with the main-paper claim that
direction-blind distances average the two failure modes together and so dilute
coverage under a backbone.

\subsection{Estimator replication: $\epsilon$-radius coverage}
Table~\ref{tab:supp_eps} replicates the coverage signal with a Monte-Carlo
$\epsilon$-radius support-overlap estimator at 1, 4, and 16\,px (thresholds
mapped into the normalized BFV space), in both the directed and the symmetric

$\epsilon$-4\,px coverage slightly \emph{edges} mean-NN on real-motion targets

$\times$ two directions), fit leave-one-source-out, overfits---within-context
$\rho{=}0.56$ in-fold but only $0.34$ held out, \emph{below} the single fit-free
distance ($0.57$), and even adding mean-NN to the six reaches just $0.45$. This is
a further instance of the learned-combiner negative control (main Sec.~\ref{sec:predict}): the
extra granularity is fitting noise, not signal.

\subsection{Where directionality does \emph{not} live: the anchor}
Directional borrowing of the level anchor $L$ (interpolating observed cells
with regime-matched directed similarities) \emph{loses} to plain symmetric
inverse-distance weighting on both anchor-interpolation problems
(held-out-benchmark MAE 16.9 symmetric vs.\ 18.9--24.1 directional;
held-out-source 8.6 vs.\ 8.7--10.1). Directionality lives where learning
happens (the ranking term $g$), not where measurement is interpolated---a
clean separation between the principle and the plumbing.

\subsection{Pretrained arm of the generated grid}
The pretrained (frozen-backbone) arm of the intervention grid spans nine
sources, but the grid was constructed to vary off-target mass, so the
missing-support distance is near-constant across these sources and any ranking
it induces is estimator-noise-limited. Its unstable point estimates nominally
favor the off-target direction ($\dtb$ $+0.64$, $\dbt$ $+0.21$, sym $+0.37$)
---the \emph{opposite} of the pretrained prediction---which we report
undiscounted rather than omit, and we do not treat this arm as a test of the
pretrained half. A coverage-varying pretrained grid (varying $\dbt$ at fixed
$\dtb$) is the natural follow-up experiment.

\subsection{BFV construct validity: full recovered parameters}
Table~\ref{tab:recovery_full} gives the complete recovered generator
parameters behind main-paper Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} and Table~\ref{tab:recovery}. The camera dolly magnitude and the object
vertical-motion fraction are the two axes that separate the regimes; the
remaining object-motion parameters (translation, rotation, number of movers)
are reported for completeness. \textbf{The known blind spot:} BFV aggregates
flow vectors and is therefore invariant to whether a given aggregate field was
produced by a moving camera or by correspondingly moving objects---a lateral
camera pan and a lateral object slide yield near-identical BFV histograms. This
is a feature of the analysis, not a failure: the descriptor recovers
\emph{aggregate} motion (the quantity that drives transfer) faithfully, and is
knowably agnostic to the camera/object factorization that produces it. A
target whose motion is dominated by camera/object ambiguity (e.g.\ a pure
lateral pan) is recovered as the corresponding object motion---the same
aggregate field, a different mechanism. Disentangling the two would require a
descriptor that encodes scene geometry, which is beyond the scope of this
motion-only study.

\section{Sampling Stability of the Estimators}
\label{supp:sampling}

We recompute every feature at flow-vector budgets from $5{\times}10^4$ to
$8{\times}10^6$ and correlate each budget's value against the largest
budget's, across all 110 train$\to$benchmark pairs. Mean-NN distances in both
directions reach $\rho \ge 0.96$ by $5{\times}10^4$ vectors and $1.000$ at
the reference budget; $\epsilon$-coverage at 4\,px behaves similarly. In
contrast, kNN-KL estimators plateau near $\rho{=}0.5$ ($T{\to}B$) and
$\rho{=}0.2$ ($B{\to}T$) and never stabilize---they measure density, which
for procedurally generated data is an artifact of the sampling budget rather
than an intrinsic property. This motivates the main paper's restriction to
support-geometry estimators. Within the near-tied intervention family, the
$\dtb$ ranking remains estimator-noise-limited even after 5-seed averaging;
out-of-sample correlations there are attenuated (conservative), and the
canonical training sets, whose spreads are an order of magnitude wider, are
unaffected.

\section{The Middlebury Exclusion}
\label{supp:mid}

During analysis we observed that for pretrained variants the Middlebury
evaluation returned $52.6 \pm 4.7$ \pck{} regardless of training source
(within-context spread 4.7 vs.\ 10--17 on other benchmarks), while
from-scratch variants spread normally (15.3). Inspection identified a defect
in our Middlebury evaluation harness; pending re-evaluation, all reported
numbers exclude Middlebury (the remaining 9 benchmarks; 99 models, 891
evaluations). Sensitivity: with Middlebury included, every headline conclusion
is unchanged---the flip statistic is $+0.82$ rather than $+0.90$, and the
policy's held-out correlations shift by at most ${\sim}0.03$; the largest
single effect of the exclusion is on the pretrained-regime oracle fraction
($0.554\!\to\!0.612$), because
near-invariant contexts are unrankable and dilute within-context averages.

\section{Additional Robustness and Disentangling Analyses}
\label{supp:newrobust}
This section collects diagnostics that probe how far the coverage law
generalizes and separate the two confounds---transfer level and
appearance---from the motion-coverage signal. All use the nine-benchmark
(Middlebury-free) table.

\subsection{Static-prior diagnostics, per training source}
The main-text static-prior table reports config means of the two input-ablation
diagnostics; Table~\ref{tab:staticprior_persource} gives the per-source values
over 37 CATs++ checkpoints (best peak-\pck{} snapshot each). We label the four
backbone configurations \emph{S-t}, \emph{S-f}, \emph{I-t}, \emph{I-f}, where
S/I denotes a scratch or ImageNet-pretrained backbone and t/f denotes a trained
or frozen encoder. The pattern is systematic: scratch encoders (S-t, S-f) sit
near the input-blind ceiling (id/real ratio $0.6$--$1.0$, source-sensitivity
${\sim}2$) on every source, while pretrained encoders (I-t, I-f) use the input
(ratio ${\le}0.5$, sensitivity $5$--$15$) wherever they are available. The
trained scratch encoder (S-t) is the more degenerate of the two scratch columns
on most sources, consistent with S-t transferring below its random-frozen twin
(S-f)---the $\Delta_{\text{enc}}<0$ result reported in the main paper.
SPair, the lone semantic source, is fully input-blind from scratch (ratio
$1.00$). Pretrained controls were unavailable for movi\_f, FlyingThings, and
Sintel.

\subsection{Off-target mass from scratch tracks encoder-degeneracy}

The encoder-degeneracy criterion itself---whether a trained-from-scratch encoder
beats a frozen random one ($\Delta_{\text{enc}}$)---is reported in the main paper,
where it underwrites every exclusion. Here we add a complementary observation:
from scratch, off-target mass ($\dtb$) carries a weak secondary signal, and it is
concentrated in the one architecture whose scratch encoder fails to learn. The table below gives, per
architecture, the within-context off-target and coverage correlations on
from-scratch real-motion cells, alongside how much training the scratch encoder
gains over a frozen random one (trained $-$ frozen):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Arch. & off-target $\rho$ & coverage $\rho$ & encoder gain (trained $-$ frozen) \\
\midrule
CATs++ & $+0.65$ & $+0.11$ & $+1.3$ (\(\approx\)random) \\
GLU-Net & $+0.19$ & $+0.64$ & $+4.6$ \\
RAFT & $+0.32$ & $+0.44$ & --- \\
FlowFormer & $+0.17$ & $+0.49$ & $+7.7$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The relationship is monotone: the more the scratch encoder actually learns, the
less off-target mass matters and the more coverage dominates. Off-target mass
``working'' from scratch is therefore the same bias signature as the
scratch-semantic floor---it appears where the model leans on a prediction prior
rather than learned features---not a second selection law. Pooled over scratch
real-motion cells it falls from $+0.33$ (all architectures) to $+0.18$ once the
encoder-degenerate CATs++ is removed, while coverage rises from $+0.42$ to
$+0.57$.

\subsection{Density is not the from-scratch--semantic signal (negative control)}
A natural alternative to the floor explanation is that from-scratch--semantic
transfer is helped by denser or more spatially-aligned supervision rather than
by motion. We tested this with source-side richness features (flow entropy,
moving-pixel fraction, log point count), a statistical partial-correlation
control, and a physical magnitude-equalization of the coverage distance. The
motion-richness features are \emph{negative} on from-scratch--semantic
($-0.38$/$-0.29$) and equally negative on from-scratch--real-motion---they track
unnatural, extreme motion (the high-entropy sources are the 2D-warp and
random-flipping variants), not a semantic-specific benefit. Spatial alignment is
a saturated non-signal for dense sources. And motion coverage on
from-scratch--semantic stays at the floor under both controls (raw $-0.20$,
statistical-partial $+0.14$, magnitude-equalized $-0.18$). Nothing source-side
cleanly ranks sources there; the floor is a floor. (Caveat: in our source set
label density is entangled with motion naturalness, so a clean causal test is a
label-density \emph{training} ablation, which we have not run.)

\subsection{Spatial vs.\ flow: decomposing the coverage distance}
BFV is four-dimensional ($x,y,\Delta x,\Delta y$), and the normalization gives
the spatial coordinates a wider range than the displacements, so the joint
nearest-neighbour distance is partly spatial. We recompute the two directed
distances in three sub-spaces of the same cloud---full, spatial-only ($x,y$),
and flow-only ($\Delta x,\Delta y$)---and re-run the stratified within-context
correlations. The clean axis is flow-coverage: it is positive wherever the
network can exploit motion (real-motion in both regimes, $+0.18$ scratch /
$+0.26$ pretrained; semantic only under a backbone, $+0.53$) and negative for
from-scratch--semantic ($-0.20$). The semantic precision signal is not a pure
spatial artifact (spatial-only off-target $\approx0$), and the spatial component
of coverage helps only in the from-scratch--semantic floor cell ($+0.43$),
exactly where flow-coverage is anti-predictive. So real-motion transfer is
predicted by motion coverage; from-scratch--semantic transfer, where it moves at
all, is predicted by spatial sampling rather than motion---reinforcing that the
floor is a place where motion is unusable.

\subsection{Is the binding direction carried by specific datasets?}
We jackknife the regime-mean directed correlations, dropping each source
(leave-one-source-out, $\times 11$) and each generator family
(leave-one-family-out, $\times 5$), and report the range of the
regime-relevant direction. The pretrained missing-support signal is
dataset-independent (range $[{+}0.57,{+}0.65]$, no family load-bearing); the

\subsection{Appearance vs.\ motion: partial correlations}

\subsection{Fixed-motion appearance: level vs.\ selection}
Four appearance variants of one camera-dominant source (mesh $\times$ texture)
share motion (flow-distance spread $\le 1.03\times$). Within each benchmark,
appearance distance orders transfer ($\rho$ up to $+0.80$)---but the
\emph{same} variant wins on every benchmark (cross-benchmark ordering
$\rho \approx +0.8$ to $+1.0$). Appearance therefore sets a target-invariant
\emph{level}, not a target-selective ranking; the within-benchmark correlation
is the level effect, since realistic texture is uniformly both better and
appearance-closer.

within the pretrained regime the gap--level association is \emph{negative}---the

\subsection{Which OOS cells are interpretable}

\section{Off-Target Mass at Fixed Coverage (the Precision Ladder)}
\label{supp:precision}
The main-paper intervention section reports that off-target mass is nearly free at
fixed coverage; Table~\ref{tab:precision} gives the full ladder. Starting from the
KITTI-recovered source, a fraction $f$ of training pairs is replaced with
horizontally-mirrored copies (mirrored frame $+$ exact mirror flow), which raises
off-target mass while holding coverage \emph{pinned} (verified BFV ladder:
$d_{B\to T}$ flat at ${\approx}0.0017$, $d_{T\to B}$ ramps $16\times$,
$0.04{\to}0.66$). $\Delta_f$ is peak \pck{} at $f{=}0.5$ minus $f{=}0$. The
configurations that learn the task lose at most $2.4$ \pck{} with half their motion
pushed off-target, and a pretrained backbone is flat; KITTI-2015 is consistent. The
from-scratch CATs++ config is degenerate (it fails the beat-random criterion of the
main-paper setup) and is excluded.
